AI Is Transforming Legal Practice in India

AI is entering Indian legal practice faster than most lawyers expect. Contract review AI (Lawgist, Kira, Luminance), research AI (Westlaw AI, SCC Online AI), court filing automation, and client intake chatbots are all being adopted by top Indian law firms. In 2026, law firms and corporate legal departments are actively seeking lawyers who understand AI tools — not to replace legal thinking, but to dramatically increase efficiency.

DPDP Act — The AI + Legal Intersection Every Lawyer Must Know

The Digital Personal Data Protection (DPDP) Act 2023 is the most important AI-adjacent legal development in India. Every organisation collecting digital data must comply — and they need legal counsel who understands both the law and the technology. Lawyers who can advise on DPDP Act implementation, consent management, and data localisation are in extremely high demand across corporate legal departments.

Frequently Asked Questions

Should lawyers learn AI in India 2026?

Yes — AI tools are actively being adopted by Indian law firms and corporate legal departments. Lawyers who can use contract review AI, legal research AI, and drafting AI tools complete work 5-10x faster. DPDP Act expertise (AI + data privacy law intersection) is particularly high-demand. Lawyers who ignore AI risk becoming less competitive in the next 3-5 years.

What AI tools are used in legal practice India?

Key AI tools in Indian legal practice 2026: Lawgist (contract review), SCC Online AI (legal research), Westlaw AI (research), ChatGPT/Claude (drafting), custom DPDP compliance tools (regulatory). Harvey.ai is US-focused but increasingly used by MNCs with India operations. Most tools work best with human oversight — lawyers review AI outputs, not replace their judgment.

Is there an IIM course on LegalTech for lawyers?

IIM Indore AI & Cybersecurity for Organizations is the closest IIM programme to legal technology — covering AI governance, data privacy (DPDP Act), and cyber risk from an organisational strategy perspective. While not specifically for lawyers, it is the most relevant IIM credential for legal professionals handling technology governance. No IIM currently offers a dedicated LegalTech programme.
